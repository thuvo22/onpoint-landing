What an accessible shower can include

Each item below is planned with you and listed in the written scope. Choose the ones that fit the people who use the room now and the way you expect to use it later.

Curbless or low-curb entry

A curbless shower floor sits level with the bathroom floor and slopes to the drain, so there is nothing to step over. When the floor structure does not allow a level entry, a low curb keeps the step small.

Blocking for grab bars

Before the walls are closed, we add solid wood blocking where grab bars go, so each bar fastens into framing instead of tile and board alone. Blocking can also go where you may want a bar later.

A built-in bench

A tiled bench gives you a place to sit while you shower. It is built and waterproofed together with the shower walls and floor.

A handheld shower

A handheld shower on a slide bar can sit within reach of the bench, beside or in place of a fixed shower head, with the controls placed where they are easy to reach.

Floor tile for footing

Small floor tile, such as a hex mosaic, has more grout lines than large tile, which helps with footing on a wet floor. We go over floor tile options with you during selections.

Linear drain and a clear entry

A linear drain along one wall lets the floor slope in one direction, which suits a curbless entry. A wide opening or a fixed glass panel instead of a door keeps the way in clear.

Accessible shower questions

How much does a curbless or accessible shower cost?

An accessible shower is built as part of a full-tile bathroom remodel. Full bathroom remodels start at $18,000, and most run $25,000 to $45,000. Room size, the floor work needed for a curbless entry, drain changes, tile and fixtures affect the price. You get a fixed written price before any work starts.

Can every bathroom have a curbless shower?

Not every bathroom. A curbless entry needs the shower floor to slope to the drain while the bathroom floor stays level, so it depends on the floor structure and where the drain can go. When a level entry does not fit the room, a low curb keeps the step small. Dillon checks this during the free in-home visit.

Do you install grab bars in tile showers?

Yes. We add solid wood blocking inside the shower walls before the backer board and tile go up, then mount the grab bars into that blocking so they are firmly anchored. We plan the bar locations with you before the walls are closed.

Can you add a shower bench and a handheld shower?

Yes. A built-in tile bench is waterproofed with the rest of the shower, and a handheld shower on a slide bar can be placed within reach of the bench. We plan the bench and fixture locations with you during selections.

What happens at the free in-home visit?

Dillon measures the room, talks through your project and budget, and gives you a price range. If the project is a fit, we plan the layout and selections with you, and you get a fixed written price before any work starts.

Can you change only the shower?
