What a tub-to-shower conversion includes

The written scope lists each of these steps for your room, so you know what the price covers before any work starts.

1. Tub removal

The tub, its surround and the old wall tile come out, and the framing, subfloor and plumbing are checked before anything new goes in.

2. Drain and valve

A tub drain and valve sit in different places than a shower's. The licensed master plumbers we work with move them for the new layout, and the change is priced in the written scope.

3. Framing and blocking

Framing for the new shower walls, a niche or a bench, plus solid wood blocking anywhere you want a grab bar now or later.

4. Waterproofing and flood test

A connected membrane on the walls and a sloped pan with a waterproof liner tied to the drain. We fill the pan and confirm it holds water for 24 hours before the shower floor tile goes in.

5. Tile

Wall tile, floor tile, the niche and any bench, laid out to the plan you approved.

6. Glass and fixtures

The glass door or panel, shower head and trim go in after the tile is finished.

Before you remove the tub

Keep a tub where you need one. If children bathe at home, keep a tub in at least one bathroom. Some homeowners convert the tub in one bathroom and keep the tub in a guest or hall bath. See our guest bathroom remodels.

Decide on the entry. A low curb or a curbless entry changes the floor work. If anyone in the house has trouble stepping over a tub wall, see our curbless and accessible showers.

Check the footprint. A shower can use the same footprint as the old tub, or extend into the space of a separate built-in tub when the room allows. Moving walls or the drain changes the scope, and Dillon goes over the options at the visit.

Tub-to-shower conversion questions

How much does a tub-to-shower conversion cost?

A tub-to-shower conversion is built as part of a full-tile bathroom remodel. Full bathroom remodels start at $18,000, and most run $25,000 to $45,000. Shower size, drain and valve changes, tile, glass and the rest of the room's scope affect the price. You get a fixed written price before any work starts.

How long does a tub-to-shower conversion take?

The schedule depends on the agreed scope, any plumbing changes and material lead times. We stage materials before demolition and agree on the construction schedule with you before work starts.

Can the new shower be larger than the old tub?

Sometimes. When the room allows, the shower can extend past the old tub footprint, for example into the space of a separate built-in tub. Moving walls or the drain changes the scope and price, and Dillon goes over the options at the visit.

Can you add grab bars, a bench or a curbless entry?

Yes. We can add wood blocking for grab bars, a built-in bench, a handheld shower and a curbless or low-curb entry when the floor allows it. See curbless and accessible showers.

Should I keep a tub somewhere in the house?

If children bathe at home, keep a tub in at least one bathroom. Some homeowners convert the tub in one bathroom and keep the tub in a guest or hall bath.

Can you convert the tub without remodeling the rest of the bathroom?

Our $18,000 minimum applies to full bathroom remodels. If you only want the tub converted, describe it when you contact us so we can confirm fit before we visit.

What happens at the free in-home visit?

Dillon measures the room, talks through your project and budget, and gives you a price range. If the project is a fit, we plan the layout and selections with you, and you get a fixed written price before any work starts.
