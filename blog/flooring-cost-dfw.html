Flooring Installation Cost Guide for DFW

By Dillon Hubbard, OnPoint Pros · April 7, 2026 · Updated October 25, 2026

A useful flooring quote covers the rooms, material and preparation together. Compare the complete scope before deciding which price is lower.

The plank price is only part of the installed cost. Removal, subfloor work, stairs, trim and furniture handling can change the total. Ask what is included, what is excluded and how concealed damage would be priced.

Start with our flooring installation services and real project photos to plan the work you want quoted.

Compare the Same Scope

  • List each room, hallway, closet and staircase, with approximate area.
  • Identify the selected flooring and whether you or the installer supplies it.
  • Separate old-floor removal and disposal from the new installation.
  • Include transitions, trim, door clearances and furniture or appliance handling.
  • Ask how additional work is approved if removal reveals damage.

Subfloor Preparation Changes the Quote

The condition of the surface underneath matters. Ask how flatness, moisture and visible damage will be checked against the selected flooring system. Not every room needs the same preparation, and flooring is not a repair for an active leak or structural movement.

The quote should identify the preparation allowed for and the process for work that cannot be assessed until the old floor comes up. Underlayment, adhesives and tile membranes depend on the chosen product and substrate; adding a premium layer is not automatically the right installation.

Compare Products, Not Just Material Names

LVP and laminate: product grade, installation method and accessories vary. Compare the actual products and room approvals rather than assuming one category is always cheapest.

Hardwood: species, construction, finish, board size and installation method affect the quote. Read our LVP and engineered hardwood comparison for the main tradeoffs.

Tile: layout, tile size, substrate preparation, setting materials and finishing details affect labor and cost.

Carpet: compare carpet, pad, seams, stairs and removal as a complete scope. The cheapest material is not always the lowest finished-project price.

Price Stairs and Transitions Separately

Ask for the number of treads, risers, landings and transitions in the quote. These details can disappear inside a whole-house square-foot price, even though they require their own material and installation plan.

Plan Around the Work

Discuss access to rooms, furniture, pets and any phases needed while you live in the home. The schedule should account for removal, preparation and the selected product. Confirm the sequence rather than relying on a generic number of days.

For an example of product-specific planning requirements, see Shaw's vinyl installation guidance. Follow the instructions for the flooring you actually choose.

Frequently Asked Questions

LVP or engineered hardwood, which should I pick?

Compare the specific products for the rooms, moisture exposure, traffic and look you want. LVP offers wood-look options; engineered hardwood has a real wood surface. The complete installed quote and care requirements matter more than a broad material ranking.

How long does a whole-house flooring install take?

The schedule depends on the measured area, removal, subfloor condition, product requirements, stairs and whether rooms must be completed in phases. Ask for a project-specific schedule before work begins.

Do you move furniture?

Tell us which furniture and appliances need to move. The written quote should state who handles them, any restrictions and the cost; do not assume moving is included in a flooring-only price.
