How to Survive Living Through a Remodel

By Dillon Hubbard, OnPoint Pros · April 7, 2026 · Updated July 9, 2026

Most DFW homeowners don't move out during a remodel. That's fine — but it requires a plan. Here's how to make the next few weeks bearable.

Set Up a Temporary Kitchen

If your kitchen is the project, you'll need a temporary kitchenette somewhere else in the house. Most clients use the dining room or laundry room. The essentials: microwave, mini-fridge, electric kettle, coffee setup, paper plates, and a dish tub.

Move pantry items in advance. Sort what you actually use weekly vs. monthly. Donate the cans you've been ignoring for two years.

Manage Dust

Demo creates dust that finds its way into every room. Talk to your contractor about plastic dust barriers at the work zone. Ask them to run an air scrubber if it's a long project.

Cover or remove anything sensitive — books, electronics, art — even in rooms not being touched. Dust travels.

Plan for Kids and Pets

Tools and chemicals are out during the day. Demo days are loud. Plan to keep kids and pets in a controlled area or out of the house during the noisiest days.

Communicate With Your Project Lead

Most stress in a remodel comes from not knowing what's happening. A daily photo from your project lead, plus the ability to text questions, prevents 90% of it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I move out for a kitchen remodel?

Most don't. With a temporary kitchenette and a dust plan, it's manageable.

What about a master bath remodel?

If it's your only bathroom, plan around the 3–5 days the toilet and shower are offline. If you have a guest bath, you're fine.

Is it worth getting a hotel for the loudest days?

For demo day in a small home, yes — it's worth a night out for sanity.
