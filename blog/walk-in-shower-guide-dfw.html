The DFW Homeowner's Guide to Walk-In Showers

By Dillon Hubbard, OnPoint Pros · July 1, 2026 · Updated July 9, 2026

A walk-in shower is the centerpiece of most bathroom remodels we do in DFW. Get the fundamentals right and it looks stunning and lasts for decades. Here's what to know before you build one.

Waterproofing Is Everything

The single most important part of a walk-in shower is the part you'll never see. We build every shower with a Schluter (or equivalent) waterproofing membrane behind the tile. Skipping or cheaping out on this step is the number-one reason DFW showers fail and have to be torn out within a few years.

Ask any contractor exactly how they waterproof. If the answer is vague or 'the tile keeps water out,' walk away — tile and grout are not waterproof.

Curbless vs. Curbed Entry

A curbed shower has a small threshold to step over and is the traditional, budget-friendly option. A curbless (zero-entry) shower is flush with the floor — sleeker, easier to clean, wheelchair-friendly, and ideal for aging in place, but it requires careful floor sloping and drainage, so it costs a bit more.

Curbless with a linear drain against the wall is the most popular high-end look we build right now.

Glass, Niches, Benches, and Drains

Glass: frameless is the cleanest look; semi-frameless saves money and still looks great. Niches: recessed shelves keep bottles off the floor — plan their height before tiling. Benches: great for shaving, seating, or resting; can be built-in or floating. Drains: a center point drain is standard and economical; a linear drain enables larger tile and curbless entry.

Decide these details before demo, not during — changing them mid-build is where surprise costs come from.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a walk-in shower cost in DFW?

It ranges widely with size, tile, glass, and features like curbless entry or a linear drain. We give a written line-item quote so you can see the cost of each upgrade and choose what's worth it to you.

Is a curbless walk-in shower a good idea?

For many homeowners, yes — it's sleek, easy to clean, and future-proof for aging in place. It requires proper floor sloping, which is why it costs a little more, but done right it's our most-requested style.

How do you keep a walk-in shower from leaking?

Full waterproofing behind the tile with a proven membrane system, a properly sloped pan, and correct drain installation. It's the invisible work that determines whether a shower lasts 5 years or 30.
