Whole-Home Painting Cost Guide for DFW

By Dillon Hubbard, OnPoint Pros · April 7, 2026 · Updated July 9, 2026

Painting prices in DFW vary wildly because most painters don't break down the actual cost drivers. Here's what's really moving your quote.

Prep Is the Real Variable

Anyone can roll a wall. The cost difference between a $4,000 paint job and a $9,000 paint job is almost always prep. Sanding, caulking, patching, masking, priming. The boring part. The part most painters skip.

When you compare two quotes, ask each painter exactly what prep is included. If one quote skips priming bare drywall, skips caulking trim lines, or skips sanding gloss between coats — that's why it's cheaper, and it's also why it'll look terrible in two years.

Cost Drivers

Square footage — the obvious one.

Number of colors — more colors mean more masking, more cuts, and more days.

Ceiling height — vaulted and 2-story ceilings need scaffolding and add real time.

Trim and door count — these are slow surfaces. Sprayed doors and trim cost more upfront but look factory-finished.

Cabinet refinishing — sprayed cabinets in a controlled spray zone are a different project from rolled cabinets and cost more for a reason.

Exterior siding type — wood, fiber-cement, brick, stucco, and aluminum all have different prep needs.

Paint product tier — Sherwin-Williams Cashmere or Benjamin Moore Aura cost more than contractor-grade lines, but hold up in DFW UV.

Where to Splurge

Premium paint. The product cost difference is small. The lifespan difference is huge.

Sprayed cabinets. Rolled cabinets show brush marks. Sprayed cabinets look like factory finish.

Exterior prep. Power washing, scraping, and caulking before paint is the difference between a 3-year exterior and a 10-year exterior.
