How to Pick a Trustworthy DFW Remodeling Contractor

By Dillon Hubbard, OnPoint Pros · April 7, 2026

Hiring a remodeling contractor in North Dallas is the highest-stakes decision in the entire project. The contractor — not the materials, not the design — determines whether your remodel finishes on time and on budget. Here's how to vet one.

Six Questions to Ask Every DFW Contractor

1. Are you fully insured and bonded? Can I see the certificates? A real contractor will email them within an hour of you asking. If they hesitate, walk away.

2. Will the same crew be on my project from start to finish? Honest answer: most won't. The ones who say yes and mean it are the ones you want.

3. Who is my single point of contact? If the answer is "call the office," walk away. You want a name and a cell number.

4. Can I see your written line-item quote format? The good ones already have a template and will show you. The bad ones will say "every project is different" and give you a single number.

5. Who pulls permits? The contractor should. Always. If they tell you to pull your own, that's a red flag — they're trying to avoid being on the hook with the city.

6. What's your warranty? Get it in writing. "We stand behind our work" is meaningless. A written workmanship warranty is not.

Red Flags

Pressure to sign today. A real contractor is busy. They don't need to close you on the first visit.

Cash-only or large deposit demands. A reasonable deposit is normal. 50% upfront is not.

No physical address. If they only have a Google Voice number and a magnet on a truck, that's a problem.

Quotes that don't itemize. See above.

Bad-mouthing other contractors. Pros don't trash competitors. Insecure contractors do.

Green Flags

They show up to the estimate on time. If they can't be on time for the sales call, what do you think will happen during the build?

They ask more questions than they answer. A good contractor wants to understand the project before quoting it.

They have references in your specific city. A contractor who's worked in your DFW neighborhood already knows your housing stock and HOA rules.

They give you a written timeline before you sign. Not a vague "four to six weeks." Real milestones.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I get multiple quotes?

Yes — usually three. But don't pick the cheapest; pick the one whose quote is the most detailed and whose communication style you trust.

How much deposit is normal?

Typically 10–25% to lock the schedule and order long-lead materials. 50%+ is a yellow flag.

What if the contractor wants payment in cash?

Walk away. Cash-only is usually a tax dodge and means you have no recourse if something goes wrong.
