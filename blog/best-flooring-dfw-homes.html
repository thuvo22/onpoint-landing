Best Flooring for DFW Homes: LVP, Hardwood, Tile & Carpet Compared

By Dillon Hubbard, OnPoint Pros · July 2, 2026 · Updated July 9, 2026

There's no single “best” floor — there's the best floor for each room, budget, and household. Here's how we help DFW homeowners choose, and why the North Texas climate should drive the decision.

Start With How the Room Is Used

Busy living areas, entries, kitchens: waterproof luxury vinyl plank (LVP) is the DFW default — it shrugs off spills, pets, and foot traffic and looks like wood.

Wet areas (bathrooms, laundry): porcelain or ceramic tile is still the gold standard for durability and water resistance.

Bedrooms and stairs: carpet is warm, quiet, and budget-friendly where water isn't a concern.

Formal spaces: engineered or solid hardwood brings warmth and resale value.

The Five Options, Honestly

Luxury Vinyl Plank (LVP): 100% waterproof, scratch-resistant, mid-range price. The best all-around value for most DFW homes.

Engineered hardwood: real wood look with better stability than solid wood in our humidity swings.

Solid hardwood: beautiful and refinishable for decades, but more sensitive to moisture and movement.

Tile: nearly indestructible and waterproof; hard and cold underfoot, higher labor cost.

Laminate: tough, wood-look, budget-friendly — but not as water-tolerant as LVP.

Carpet: cheapest and coziest for bedrooms; not for wet or high-traffic zones.

Why North Texas Soil Matters

DFW's expansive clay soil moves as it wets and dries, and that movement travels up into your slab. A floor installed over an unlevel, moving subfloor develops gaps, squeaks, and cracks no matter how good the material is.

That's why the material you pick matters less than whether the installer levels the subfloor first. Get both right and any of these floors will last.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most popular flooring in DFW right now?

Waterproof luxury vinyl plank (LVP). It looks like hardwood, survives kids and pets, and costs less than real wood — which is why it's on most of the homes we floor.

Is LVP or laminate better for DFW homes?

LVP, in most cases. Both are wood-look and budget-friendly, but LVP is fully waterproof while laminate can swell if water sits on it — a real advantage in Texas homes with kids and pets.

Can you mix flooring types in one house?

Yes, and we recommend it — LVP or hardwood in living areas, tile in wet rooms, carpet in bedrooms. We match transitions cleanly so it looks intentional, not patchworked.
