Best Flooring for DFW Homes: LVP, Hardwood, Tile & Carpet Compared

By Dillon Hubbard, OnPoint Pros · July 2, 2026 · Updated October 25, 2026

The right floor depends on the room, product, budget and household. Compare how you want it to look and feel with the preparation and care it needs.

Choose flooring room by room, then plan the whole installation. Check each product's approved use, moisture requirements and care instructions. No material is a substitute for dealing with a leak, damaged substrate or structural movement.

See our flooring installation services for the scope we can discuss, or compare completed flooring transformations.

Start With How the Room Is Used

  • Living rooms, entries and kitchens: compare wear, cleaning, moisture exposure and the look you want across connected rooms.
  • Bathrooms and laundry rooms: choose a product and installation system approved for that use. A bathroom floor and a shower floor are different applications.
  • Bedrooms: compare softness, sound, cleaning and how the floor connects to the hallway.
  • Stairs: plan treads, risers, nosings and landings as a complete system.

Compare the Flooring Options

Luxury vinyl plank: wood-look options with product-specific wear and water-resistance claims. Scratches and dents are still possible; review care and warranty limits.

Engineered hardwood: a real wood surface over a layered core. Suitability and refinishing options depend on the construction, wear layer and condition.

Solid hardwood: a real wood option with its own installation, indoor-condition and maintenance requirements. A DFW address alone does not rule it in or out.

Tile: compare finish, layout, grout, substrate and the installation system. Tile alone does not establish a waterproof floor assembly.

Laminate: compare the actual wear surface, wet-area approval and installation instructions; products within the category differ.

Carpet: compare texture, pad, cleaning and the intended room. Cost depends on the full product and installation scope.

Check What Is Under the Old Floor

Removal may expose damage, old adhesive or uneven areas that were not visible beforehand. Ask what preparation is included and how additional work would be priced and approved. Do not cover an unresolved moisture or structural issue with new flooring.

Bring the Whole Project Into One Quote

  • List connected rooms, hallways, closets and stairs, with approximate measurements.
  • Identify your current floor and the material you are considering.
  • Confirm removal, disposal, trim, transitions and furniture or appliance handling.
  • Ask about room access and installation phases while you live in the home.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I choose flooring for a busy household?

Start with the rooms, traffic, pets, moisture exposure and care you can keep up with. Compare the selected products, installation requirements and warranty exclusions rather than assuming one material is best for every home.

Is LVP or laminate better for DFW homes?

Compare the specific products. Water-resistance claims, approved rooms, wear surfaces and installation requirements vary within both categories. Neither material fixes moisture or damage underneath the floor.

Can you mix flooring types in one house?

Yes. Plan the materials room by room, then discuss floor heights, door clearances, stairs and transitions together so the installation works as a whole.

How much does it cost to replace carpet with LVP on stairs?

One published North DFW project example started from $5,100 for treads, risers, a landing, carpet removal and disposal. It is a scope example, not a standard staircase price; your dimensions, selected products and preparation determine the quote.

Does my slab need leveling before new floors?

The subfloor needs to be checked against the selected flooring requirements. Correct uneven areas where needed; do not assume every slab needs the same treatment or that surface preparation repairs structural movement.

Is LVP or hardwood better for a Texas house?

Choose for the room, product and care requirements. LVP offers wood-look options, while hardwood provides a real wood surface. Moisture conditions, installation method and refinishing options must be checked for the specific product.

What if there is damage under my old floor?

Ask how concealed damage will be assessed, priced and approved before additional work begins. The quote should explain known preparation and how newly exposed issues will be handled.
