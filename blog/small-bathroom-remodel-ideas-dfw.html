Small Bathroom Remodel Ideas That Actually Work in DFW Homes

By Dillon Hubbard, OnPoint Pros · July 1, 2026 · Updated July 9, 2026

A small bathroom is where smart choices matter most — every inch and every finish counts. Here are the moves that make a compact DFW guest bath or powder room feel bigger and more expensive than it is.

Make the Space Feel Bigger

Large-format tile, fewer grout lines. Big tiles read as more open than lots of small ones. Running them to the ceiling draws the eye up.

A floating vanity. Seeing the floor continue underneath the vanity makes the room feel larger and is easier to clean around.

A frameless or glass shower panel. Instead of a curtain or framed door, glass keeps sightlines open across the whole room.

Lighting and Mirrors

Swap a single builder-grade fixture for layered lighting — a good vanity light plus a bright, high-CRE overhead. A large mirror (or mirrored medicine cabinet for storage) bounces light and doubles the sense of space.

Warm, dimmable LEDs make a small bath feel like a retreat instead of a closet.

Storage Without the Bulk

Recessed niches in the shower, a mirrored medicine cabinet, and a vanity with real drawers (not just a cabinet door) get everything off the counter. In a powder room, a wall-mounted or pedestal-adjacent shelf keeps the footprint tiny.

The goal in a small bath is always the same: clear counters, open floor, and one or two high-quality finishes instead of a lot of cheap ones.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it worth remodeling a small bathroom in DFW?

Yes — small baths have a high return because the square footage is low but the impact on daily use and home appeal is high. A guest-bath refresh is one of the most cost-effective updates you can make.

What's the best flooring for a small DFW bathroom?

Large-format porcelain tile or quality luxury vinyl plank both look great and survive moisture. We usually steer clients away from natural stone in bathrooms because it stains and needs sealing.

Can you remodel a small bathroom without moving plumbing?

Often, yes — keeping the toilet, vanity, and shower in place keeps the project affordable. We only recommend moving plumbing when the existing layout truly doesn't work.
